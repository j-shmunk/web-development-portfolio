Mountain Laurel (Kalmia Latifolia)


Description:

Pennsylvania’s official state flower, this slow-growing evergreen shrub is celebrated for its twisted, gnarled branches and glossy dark green foliage. In late spring, it produces large, showy clusters of unique star-shaped or cup-shaped blooms ranging from pure white to deep pink, complete with intricate purple markings. It provides crucial cover for forest wildlife throughout the winter months.


Growing Requirements:

  • Sun: Partial shade to full shade.
  • Soil: Cool, highly acidic, organic-rich, well-drained
  • Water: Moderate (requires good drainage to prevent root rot)


Eastern Redbud (Cercis Canadensis)


Description:

A beloved understory tree that signals the arrival of spring as cluster upon cluster of tiny, pea-like magenta blossoms burst directly out of its bark and branches. As the vibrant bloom fades, rich heart-shaped leaves emerge, turning a soft pale yellow in autumn. Its compact size and graceful spreading canopy make it a standout choice for native residential landscapes.


Growing Requirements:

  • Sun: Full sun to partial shade
  • Soil: Rich, Moist, well-drained loams; adaptable to clay
  • Water: Moderate; establish regular watering early, highly drought-tolerant once mature


Black-Eyed Susan (Rudbeckia Hirta)


Description:

A quintessential native wildflower that adds brilliant color to meadows and garden beds from mid-summer straight through early autumn. Featuring prominent dark-brown central disks framed by intense golden-yellow petals, this hardy plant acts as an indispensable nectar hub for bees and butterflies while its seed heads feed native songbirds during the winter.


Growing Requirements:

  • Sun: Full sun (at least 6 hours daily)
  • Soil: Average, dry-to-medium moisture, well-drained soils
  • Water: Low to moderate; exceptionally heat and drought tolerant


Sugar Maple (Acer Saccharum)


Description:

A commanding canopy tree that forms the backbone of Pennsylvania's deciduous forests. Famous for its broad, palmate leaves that transform into spectacular fiery shades of yellow, orange, and deep crimson in autumn, this long-lived native provides vital shelter for nesting birds and serves as the classic historical source for traditional maple syrup production.


Growing Requirements:

  • Sun: Full sun to partial shade
  • Soil: Deep, moist, fertile, acidic soils
  • Water: Moderate; prefers consistently moist conditions and protection from harsh road salt

Wild Blue Phlox (Phlox Divaricata)


Description:

A gentle woodland perennial that slowly spreads to form breathtaking ground-cover carpets beneath emerging tree canopies in spring. Its delicate, five-petaled blooms release a sweet fragrance into the spring air, displaying subtle shades from pale lavender to periwinkle blue that attract long-tongued pollinators, swallowtail butterflies, and bumblebees.


Growing Requirements:

  • Sun: Partial shade to deep woodland shade
  • Soil: Rich, moist, humus-y, well-drained soils
  • Water: Moderate; enjoys consistent moisture similar to a natural forest floor
