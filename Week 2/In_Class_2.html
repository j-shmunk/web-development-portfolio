The Butterfly Weed (Asclepias Tuberosa)

Home Grow

What is Butterfly Weed?

Butterfly Weed is a long-lived, bushy, native herbaceous perennial known for its clusters of vibrant bright orange to yellow-orange flowers. It features dark green, lance-shaped leaves along hairy stems. Unlike most other species in the milkweed family, Asclepias tuberosa contains clear sap rather than a thick milky sap. It develops a deep taproot, making it very drought-tolerant once established, though difficult to transplant.

Plant Height and Spread

  • Height: 1 to 3 feet (30-90cm)
  • Spread: 1 to 2 feet (30-60cm)

Native Range

Eastern and central North America, ranging from Ontario and Newfoundland south to Florida, and west to Texas and Colorado. It typically grows in dry prairies, open fields, roadsides, and open woods.

Wildlife Benefits

  • Larval Host: Serves as a vital host plant for Monarch butterfly caterpillars (Danaus plexippus), Queen butterfly caterpillars, and Gray Hairstreak larvae.
  • Nectar Source: Provides nectar for adult butterflies, native bees, honeybees, beneficial wasps, and hummingbirds.
  • Deer & Rabbit Resistance: High natural resistance to deer and browsing herbivores.
